Risk-Conditioned Execution Governance for Balanced Robustness in Windy Suspended-Payload UAV Transport}

\begin{abstract}
Suspended-payload UAV transport in strong wind is sensitive to how aggressively
motion commands are executed. Even with payload-aware planning, MPC, and
low-level control, the same planned motion can be too aggressive or too
conservative under different wind and mission conditions. We propose a
stack-compatible risk-conditioned execution governor that adjusts command
execution through the existing speed scale and acceleration scale interface,
while leaving the planner/MPC/controller stack unchanged. To avoid a
misleading single aggregate, we evaluate strong-wind behavior under a
single-goal mission protocol (goal repeat = 1) and a goal-reissue stress
protocol (goal repeat = 10). The results reveal strong protocol specialists:
Wind-Level 0.85 has the highest observed single-goal count with 26/30
strict-valid runs, whereas Fixed Scale 0.80 has the highest observed stress
count with 24/30 strict-valid runs. Among the evaluated learned /
risk-conditioned methods, Risk Adapter v1 is the most balanced, achieving the
highest mean valid count (24.0/30), the highest worst-protocol count (23/30),
and the lowest total regret (2) relative to the protocol oracles. Invalid-only
failure groups and representative traces show that aggregate success can hide
distinct failure modes. These results support balanced robustness analysis
under the tested protocols, without claiming statistical significance, formal
safety, or broad hardware transfer.
\end{abstract}
