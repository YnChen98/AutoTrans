\section{Conclusion}

This paper studies risk-conditioned execution governance for windy
suspended-payload UAV transport. The proposed governor leaves the planner,
payload MPC, and SO3 controller unchanged while adapting execution
aggressiveness through \texttt{speed\_scale} and
\texttt{acceleration\_scale}. The protocol-split evaluation shows strong
specialists: \texttt{windlevel\_s085} leads the single-goal mission protocol
and \texttt{fixed\_s080} leads the goal-reissue stress protocol. Across the
two tested protocols, \texttt{risk\_adapter\_v1} provides the best balanced
learned / risk-conditioned result by mean valid count, worst-protocol count,
and total regret. Failure-group and representative-trace analyses further show
why aggregate success alone is insufficient. The current evidence supports a
bounded balanced-robustness claim under the tested protocols, without claiming
statistical significance, certified safety, learned uniform domination, or a
final \texttt{risk\_adapter\_v22} method.
