\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Suspended-Payload UAV Transport and Control}

Suspended-payload UAV transport has been studied through coupled-load
trajectory generation, integrated planning and control stacks, online optimal
trajectory generation, predictive payload control, and geometric quadrotor
control. Differential-flatness and hybrid-system formulations provide an early
foundation for cable-suspended load trajectory generation
\cite{Sreenath2013DifferentiallyFlatHybrid}, while integrated frameworks such
as AutoTrans combine planning and control for autonomous payload transportation
\cite{Li2023AutoTrans}. Real-time optimal trajectory generation has also been
used for obstacle avoidance with suspended loads
\cite{Son2020ObstacleAvoidanceSuspendedLoad}, and predictive control has been
applied to aerial payload transport with swing-related constraints
\cite{UrbinaBrito2021PredictivePayloadTransport}. Low-level quadrotor tracking
is commonly grounded in geometric SE(3) / SO(3) control
\cite{Lee2010GeometricTrackingSE3}, and swing attenuation remains an important
transport-performance concern \cite{GuerreroSanchez2017SwingAttenuation}. In
contrast to replacing these planner, MPC, or controller components, this paper
studies a complementary execution-governance layer that modulates runtime
aggressiveness through \texttt{speed\_scale} and
\texttt{acceleration\_scale}.

\subsection{Runtime Governors and Safety-Filter Boundary}

Runtime governance methods provide a conceptual precedent for modifying
references or commands around an existing controller. Reference and command
governors are commonly framed as add-on schemes for constrained or
pre-stabilized systems \cite{Garone2017ReferenceCommandGovernorsSurvey}, with
explicit reference governors extending this idea to constrained nonlinear
systems \cite{Garone2016ExplicitReferenceGovernor}. Robust explicit reference
governors have also been studied in UAV settings \cite{Nicotra2016UAVRobustERG},
and action governors provide another supervisory view of modifying nominal
control signals \cite{Li2021ActionGovernor}. Safety-filter literature gives a
stronger certified-control boundary for runtime intervention
\cite{Hsu2024SafetyFilterUnifiedView}. The governor in this paper is empirical:
it adapts execution scales through the existing command-adaptation interface
and does not provide formal invariance, stability, or safety guarantees.

\subsection{Learning-Enhanced Aerial Robustness}

Learning has been used to improve aerial robustness through risk-aware control,
hybrid aerodynamic modeling, data-driven model predictive control, temporal
learning of dynamics, and disturbance prediction. Risk-aware active learning
has been explored for quadcopter control
\cite{Andersson2017RiskAwareActiveLearning}, while hybrid aerodynamic models
and data-driven MPC show how learned models can complement model-based aerial
control \cite{Bauersfeld2021NeuroBEM,Torrente2021DataDrivenMPC}.
Physics-inspired temporal learning has also been integrated with MPC for
trajectory tracking \cite{Saviolo2022PITCN}, and hybrid model-based /
data-driven disturbance prediction has been used for precise quadrotor
tracking \cite{Lee2024HybridDisturbancePrediction}. The present work follows
this broader learning-enhanced robustness direction but uses learned risk
information in a lightweight execution-governor layer rather than replacing
the planner, MPC, or low-level controller.

\subsection{Benchmarking, Stress Testing, and Failure Analysis}

Robotics evaluation depends on the choice of measurement target and protocol:
benchmark scores can become misleading when they are treated as general
capability rather than context-dependent evidence
\cite{Muller2019MeasureTargetConfusion}. Stress testing provides a
complementary way to expose failures that may not appear under nominal
evaluation \cite{Koren2018AdaptiveStressTesting,Nalic2020StressTestingScenarioBasedADS}.
Failure taxonomies can organize observed failures and their consequences
\cite{Cameron2024DomesticRobotFailureOutcomes}. In this paper, protocol-split
success counts and invalid-only failure groups are used as diagnostic evidence
under the tested protocols, not as proof of exact physical root cause.
